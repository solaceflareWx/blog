## about the site:

welcome to my website, happy to have you! on this site i do many things. my main interest is in patterns and data corresponding with severe weather and using those patterns to predict severe weather and warn people before situations become deadly. my case studies do just that. they look at large scale meteorological events and see exactly how the formed, and when exactly it was noticed the weather would turn. 

then in data collecting, i want to take this data and start looking for those patterns, both in past and in present day, 

for the daily forecast part, where i will have a more meteorological faced forecast talking about upper airs and other more detailed views of the next few days with data publicly available as well as my own taken in MN, and a more public forecast that's just as informative without having to have a degree to understand it. my goal is to give people the tools to learn the weather and to make weather accessible for everyone.  

my WIPS and update parts is a log of how the projects I am working on are going and updates on my ideas for how to go about something. i want this to show my work in real time and allow people to understand my goals. I'm hoping to reach out and build connections with people who have a passion for spreading reliable data and information but that line of communication will not be open just yet through this site as i am just starting it. that being said, i have trustworthy in person sources to help me at this time with any questions i do have and i look forward to speaking with more people as i grow.

## now that I've introduced the site a bit, i would like to introduce the person behind it:

my name is Solace, and a lot of people compare me to a ball of sunshine, hence the name, SolaceFlare. i am a first year meteorology student and I've always been passionate about weather. I've seen a lot of cool weather phenomenon from living in two states with EF5 tornados and having an EF3 hit the military base i grew up on 6 months before said EF5 hit the state, to  lenticular clouds on mount. Rainier, I've fallen in love with weather in all its power and beauty. ive seen it tear people apart and bring people together.

i know I'm not the only weather lover out there, and i know as a collective, if people work together, from professional meteorologists who have been studying for years to self proclaimed weather nerds who are just learning how to read a rain gauge, we can build a place to put our minds together and share the data we are getting, world wide. 

## my mission and end goal:

the NWS has had its budget stripped and we are seeing the effects of it, from unwarned tornados to more severe weather going missed, people are getting hurt and i believe that working together, and having each others back is a good place to start, and with weather events getting worse and more frequent, its also the best time to start. 

call this blind optimism but i know that if people know how to do good, they will. humans have an amazing ability to care, and with the right tools, they can care enough to change the world. we can fix the data deficits because I'm sure everywhere, from big cities like Seattle and Minneapolis, to small towns like Solvang in California and Beacon in new York there is at least one person looking at the weather. maybe its the next hit meteorologist who is going to get data we as a group could only dream about, or maybe its someone whose looking at the clouds, learning how to use a barometer for the first time. 

everyday people who are care, because yes, one person from Minnesota and one from Seattle cant show a pattern, but when more people come together? that's where the magic happens. that magic is what i believe is possible, and that magic? that's my professional and personal goal. 

i want to make a place where anyone can provide data and proof of that data can share it. that data gets shared to others, with the more information gathered the more accurate the forecasts and alerts are. a place where people do what they already do: share weird weather photos and ask "what is this?" and learn about the answer.
